# Recommendation Letter — Prof. Dawn Song for Yuanbo Pang

*Final version (2026-11-21). Supersedes the earlier working draft.*

---

To the Graduate Admissions Committee:

I am writing to offer my highest recommendation for Yuanbo Pang for admission to your Ph.D. program. Yuanbo has been a research assistant in my group at the Berkeley Center for Responsible, Decentralized Intelligence (RDI) and the Berkeley Artificial Intelligence Research (BAIR) Lab since October 2025. Among the undergraduate researchers I have advised at UC Berkeley, he ranks in the top 1–2%. What truly sets him apart is a rare combination of sharp research taste and extraordinary engineering execution—he already operates with the independence, systems mastery, and scientific judgment of a strong mid-stage Ph.D. student.

Yuanbo first distinguished himself as a co-first author and core contributor on *Agents' Last Exam (ALE)*, a large-scale effort involving over 300 authors that was accepted to NeurIPS 2026. He served as a key engineering backbone for the project, owning the core infrastructure behind the benchmark: task and environment pipelines, computer-use and tool interfaces, a scalable GCP execution framework, and programmatic evaluators for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). During evaluations with frontier model teams—including OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, Qwen, and Seed—he also contributed to analyzing trajectories and identifying concrete failure modes.

Building on this work, Yuanbo stepped up as the first author of *Software Last Exam (SLE)*, a flagship track of *ALE V2* currently under review at CVPR 2027. SLE is a clear reflection of both his research taste and his ability to execute complex systems at scale. Rather than chasing incremental benchmarks, he targeted a fundamental, high-impact question: whether coding models can reconstruct professional software end-to-end from documentation and observable behavior alone, without access to source code or native executables. Turning that vision into reality required formidable engineering—he independently designed and scaled the entire evaluation pipeline across over 80 open-source and proprietary applications in 20 domains (including CAD, structural simulation, 3D animation, and circuit design), building verifiable native-execution oracles that check internal state transitions, regional GUI changes, and runtime performance. Evaluating nine frontier model configurations, he showed that current systems still rarely succeed at the full-application level and rigorously diagnosed the underlying failure modes—providing actionable insights into the core bottlenecks of current coding models and RLVR training.

Beyond leading SLE, Yuanbo is also a core contributor to another *ALE V2* submission under review at CVPR 2027, *Agents' Last Exam for Media (ALE-MEDIA)*. What unifies his work across SLE, media, and game environments is a distinctive, data-driven research taste: rather than treating these as isolated benchmarks, he uses them as a systematic lens to probe and advance how general agents understand 3D space across both virtual and physical worlds. In our research discussions, I have consistently been impressed by how quickly he cuts to the core of an open-ended problem and translates ambitious ideas into concrete, verifiable experimental plans.

Yuanbo combines the research taste to identify consequential problems with the engineering firepower to build and validate them at scale. Grounded, collaborative, and relentless about getting the details right, he is fully prepared to excel in a top Ph.D. program. I recommend him without reservation.

Sincerely,

Dawn Song

Professor, Department of Electrical Engineering and Computer Sciences

University of California, Berkeley
