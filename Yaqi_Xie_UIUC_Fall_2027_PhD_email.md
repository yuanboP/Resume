My name is Yuanbo Pang, and I am an EECS undergraduate at UC Berkeley expecting to graduate in December 2026. I was especially interested in your work on UniPred and pySpatial, which connects foundation-model reasoning with grounded robot data and 3D spatial understanding. This closely aligns with my interest in building embodied agents that can reason over their physical environments, generalize to new tasks, and be evaluated reliably.

At Berkeley RDI/BAIR, I am a co-first author and core contributor to Agents' Last Exam (ALE, arXiv:2606.05405), a large-scale benchmark spanning 1K+ long-horizon, real-world agent tasks across 55 subdomains. I helped build the end-to-end benchmark and evaluation system that runs agents on executable, expert-designed tasks, automatically scores their performance with programmatic evaluators, and produces verifiable rewards for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). My work spans task and environment construction, computer-use and tool interfaces, scalable execution, and automated grading. I am currently working with frontier model teams, including OpenAI, Meta TBD Lab, Qwen, Seed, and Anthropic, on agent evaluation using ALE. This experience has given me a strong understanding of how to turn individual tasks into a systematic benchmark and where frontier agents fail in realistic, long-horizon settings.

I am now bringing this experience into robotics. Working with Zhuo Xu and Professor Masayoshi Tomizuka, I am helping build a large-scale robot-learning benchmark and currently focus on cleaning and curating egocentric human-manipulation and interaction data, including datasets such as EgoVerse, EgoDex, and HOI4D. This work has given me direct exposure to the challenges of turning heterogeneous human demonstrations into reliable, usable training data for robot learning.

I also have hands-on experience with embodied systems and perception. At Starbot, I worked on integrating vision-language-action models and agentic workflows for robot planning and control in a delivery system demonstrated at CES 2026. In my Berkeley EECS URAP research, I am developing zero-shot UAV-to-UAV detection and optical-flow-based multi-object tracking under high-speed motion and strong ego-motion, targeting a CVPR submission. In parallel, as a founding engineer at Equile, I build sandboxed agent runtimes with multi-harness execution, tool/MCP interfaces, and reproducible task environments.

Across these projects, I have worked on both sides of the problem: building and evaluating capable AI agents, and preparing the data and perception systems needed to ground them in the physical world. I am excited by the possibility of bringing this combined background to your research on adaptive and trustworthy robot partners.

I would be grateful for the opportunity to discuss potential research fit and Fall 2027 PhD opportunities in your group. I have attached my CV and transcript for your consideration.
